## Design notes

- **SPH, exactly conservative.** Within each substep every impulse comes from the start-of-substep state, so the result doesn't depend on processing order, and a symmetric start stays symmetric. Each impulse's work, at the mean of the old and new velocities, is moved between kinetic and thermal energy. Summed, that is exactly the change in kinetic energy. Applying impulses one pair at a time with their own energy exchange was tried first: it sloshed thermal energy ±15% between neighbours each substep and blew up.
- **Walls are mirrors.** Each parcel near a surface gets a ghost on the far side, seen by parcels on that face, plus a corner ghost where two faces meet. A ghost's push acts on the parcel and, equal and opposite along the same line, on the body, so angular momentum is exact. A single mirror of the parcel itself was not enough, because a parcel next to a wall loses about nine neighbours. A continuum (kernel-integral) wall was exact for smooth gas but left a 57,000 m/s² imbalance in the second layer of a lattice.
- **Kernel.** Wendland C2 for both density and pressure. Mixing poly6 density with a spiky gradient made the lattice unstable. With h = 2.5 spacings, wall pressure came out 3.7% low (an SPH discretisation effect). With h = 3 it is 0.9% low.
- **Viscosity is capped** at the impulse that stops a pair closing. Uncapped, a lone parcel hitting its own close mirror at 735 m/s got about 2×10⁵ m/s of kick.
- **Thermal energy never goes negative.** A substep that would drive a parcel below zero is retried at half the length, up to 20 times. A ghost's work is split between the parcel and the ghost's source parcel in proportion to their pressures, as for a real pair. Charging it all to the parcel drained cold parcels next to hot ones.
- **Gas pushes a free assembly as one rigid body.** Its parts get the rigid change in velocity, so a gas-filled box made of welded walls behaves as one box. The joints therefore don't see gas pressure as load; see below.
- **Bodies move with the gas through the step.** After the rigid-body integrator, each unit is rewound to its start-of-step pose and drifted forward with every gas substep, so walls sweep through the gas instead of jumping. With walls frozen at the end-of-step pose, a box moving at 100 m/s lost 91 of 100 parcels in 30 steps (found by Codex review); now it keeps them all.
- **World boundary.** Gas past `world.bounds` (default ±1,000 m) is removed. The ledger's new `boundary` source records the mass, momentum, angular momentum and energy it carried (GAS-007).
- **PRF-003.** A fill that would exceed `limit.gasParcels` is refused as a whole and logged as a `limit.refused` event. Merging parcels is not implemented yet.

## GAS-008: 2,000 parcels in real time — not met

Measured in Node 24 on this CI-class container (one core). The browser is similar.

| Workload | Parcels | Substeps per step | ms per step | Speed vs real time |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| SC-02, 300 K | 484 | 22 | 38 | 0.11× |
| SC-03, 1,000 K | 1,024 | 60–160 | 37 (average over the burn) | 0.11× |
| SC-02, 300 K | 2,025 | 44 | 230 | 0.018× |

Real time is set by physics, not by code. 300 K air has a sound speed of 347 m/s. Resolving gas features about 0.1 m across needs a substep of about 0.25·h/c ≈ 10⁻⁴ s, which is about 44 substeps per 1/240 s step at 2,000 parcels. That is roughly 1.2 million pair interactions per step: about 50× too many for single-threaded JavaScript. Options, none chosen yet:

1. **Accept slow motion for gas** (current behaviour). The sandbox spends at most 12 ms of each frame stepping, so heavy scenes run smoothly at about 0.1× instead of freezing. The time-scale readout shows the speed achieved.
2. **Scale down the speed of sound** in the `sandbox` profile, e.g. treat temperatures as 100× lower for dynamics. That is 10× fewer substeps, but rocket exhaust speeds are no longer physical.
3. **Fewer, larger parcels** (DEC-06): 0.2 m parcels instead of 0.05 m. That is about 4× fewer parcels and 2× fewer substeps, at lower resolution.
4. **Move the gas step into a Web Worker or WASM** with SIMD. Perhaps 3–10×. This is a larger engineering task, best done before M4 if real-time flight with gas is needed.

RS-00 does not yet include 2,000 parcels (§11.2, "from M3"). That waits on this decision, so that the scenario test suite stays under its 60 s budget (TST-003).

## Not done in M3 (should-level, or carried over)

- **GAS-008:** see above.
- **Joint loads from gas pressure.** Gas pushes free assemblies rigidly, so a pressurised box's welds report no load. M5 (damage, leaks) needs this. The fix is to apply gas impulses to individual parts before the joint solve.
- **Pairs across thin walls.** Parcels on opposite faces of the same body don't interact. A wall thinner than h still lets parcels near *different* bodies' far faces see each other.
- **Parcel merging** (GAS-007 "or merged"). No merging yet, so nothing to record.
- **ASM-007 / ORC-005, ARC-008, NFR-002:** carried over from M2.

## Sandbox additions

- **Gas tool (G).** Drag a rectangle to fill it at the temperature, density, parcel spacing and species set in the panel. The panel shows the resulting pressure and sound speed. *Clear* mode removes the gas in a rectangle. Both are recorded edits, so undo, replay and export work.
- **Parcels are coloured by temperature** on a log scale from 50 K to 3,000 K, using a colour-blind-safe ramp after inferno. A switch colours them by composition. The colour scale is always shown while there is gas (SBX-035).
- **Hover** a parcel to read its species, temperature, mass and speed. The inspector's Gas section shows totals, the substep count and how much gas has left the world.
- **The picker** lists SC-01, SC-02 and SC-03 first, then RS-07 with the other references. SC-03 opens following the box.
